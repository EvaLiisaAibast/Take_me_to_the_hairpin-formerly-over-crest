## Factions (classic WRC politics)

### Factory Teams

*Success comes from tradition, hierarchy, and controlled environments. Racing should be about engineering excellence, not individual heroics.*

- **Strengths:** Unlimited resources, Technical superiority, Institutional knowledge
- **Weaknesses:** Bureaucratic, Risk-averse, Slow to adapt
- **Internal contradiction:** They claim to value innovation but fire anyone who deviates from established procedures. They preach teamwork but reward individual engineers who claim credit for collective work.
- **Who benefits / who suffers:** Corporate engineers,Sponsors,Shareholders vs Privateer teams,Creative mechanics,Young drivers

### Privateers

*Racing is about human skill and adaptation, not corporate budgets. Anyone with talent should have a chance, regardless of funding.*

- **Strengths:** Flexibility, Innovation, Driver-focused
- **Weaknesses:** Limited resources, Inconsistent reliability, Financial instability
- **Internal contradiction:** They preach freedom but often rely on factory cast-off parts. They claim to be about talent but still need money to compete.
- **Who benefits / who suffers:** Talented but underfunded drivers,Creative mechanics,Local racing communities vs Sponsors seeking guaranteed exposure,Engineers who prefer structure,Risk-averse investors

### The Progressives

*Racing must evolve responsibly. Speed without responsibility is meaningless. Safety and innovation should be balanced with human cost.*

- **Strengths:** Technical innovation, Safety expertise, Regulatory influence
- **Weaknesses:** Perceived as controlling, Slower acceptance of risk, Sometimes overcautious
- **Internal contradiction:** They advocate for safety but profit from building safer cars. They claim to protect drivers but sometimes prioritize their own regulatory authority over actual driver input.
- **Who benefits / who suffers:** Safety engineers,Insurance companies,Regulatory bodies vs Purist drivers,Traditionalists,Fans who love danger

### The Old Guard

*Racing is about danger, bravery, and the absolute limit. Safety regulations have made it sterile. Real racing requires the possibility of death.*

- **Strengths:** Experience, Historical knowledge, Racing purity
- **Weaknesses:** Resistant to change, Disregard for safety, Limited modern technical knowledge
- **Internal contradiction:** They claim to value courage but often take unnecessary risks that endanger others. They preach tradition but many of them made their careers by breaking with tradition.
- **Who benefits / who suffers:** Purist fans,Historians,Drivers who thrive on danger vs Safety advocates,Modern engineers,Drivers with families

### The Media Machine

*Racing exists to entertain. Competition is secondary to the story. Dramatic conflicts, personalities, and narratives matter more than raw performance.*

- **Strengths:** Public influence, Storytelling ability, Marketing power
- **Weaknesses:** Sensationalism, Distortion of truth, Manipulation
- **Internal contradiction:** They claim to serve the fans but often create artificial drama. They preach authenticity but manufacture narratives for maximum engagement.
- **Who benefits / who suffers:** Sponsors,Casual fans,Drivers who court media attention vs Private drivers,Teams who avoid drama,Purist racing enthusiasts
